The Khmer

Angkor Wat, located in Siem Reap, Cambodia, is the largest religious complex in the world. Spanning over 402 acres (162.6 hectares), this architectural marvel was constructed in the first half of the 12th century by Khmer King Suryavarman II. Initially built as a Hindu temple dedicated to the god Vishnu and serving as the king’s personal mausoleum, it gradually transitioned into a Theravada Buddhist temple towards the end of the 12th century. It remains an active site of Buddhist pilgrimage and stands as the ultimate symbol of Cambodian national pride, taking center stage on the country's national flag.

When people look for a "Khmer museum," they are typically referring to one of two definitive, world-class institutions that preserve the extraordinary heritage of the Khmer Empire: the National Museum of Cambodia in Phnom Penh or the Angkor National Museum in Siem Reap. Depending on which city you are visiting, each provides a distinctly powerful immersion into ancient Khmer art, history, and spiritual traditions.
